# Epstein Files: Current Information and Sources

**Last checked: October 18, 2026.** This English-language reference summarizes public releases and recent developments. It is a dated snapshot and is not automatically updated.

## Current status

- **October 16, 2026 — Further disclosure remains contested.** Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison's office announced support for the proposed Epstein Files Transparency Act II, identifying H.R. 9694 and S. 5005. The proposal would give state attorneys general, survivors, and other specified parties additional ways to challenge unlawful withholding of records. The office argues that DOJ's disclosures remain incomplete and raises concerns about exposed victim information. These are the coalition's stated concerns; the announcement is not evidence that the proposal has become law. [Minnesota Attorney General, October 16, 2026](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Office/Communications/2026/10/16_EFTA.asp).
- **September 11, 2026 — Date displayed by the official library.** The DOJ Epstein Library currently shows this as its last update. DOJ says it will update the site if it identifies additional documents for release. A website update date does not establish when every individual record was published. [DOJ Epstein Library](https://www.justice.gov/epstein).
- **January 30, 2026 — Major DOJ release.** DOJ announced more than three million additional pages, bringing its reported total to nearly 3.5 million pages, alongside more than 2,000 videos and 180,000 images in that release. Those figures describe the January announcement, not a verified October total. DOJ described the production as compliance with the Epstein Files Transparency Act; the October statement above disputes the completeness of disclosure. [DOJ release announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-publishes-35-million-responsive-pages-compliance-epstein-files).

## What is publicly available?

The official [DOJ disclosures index](https://www.justice.gov/epstein/doj-disclosures) lists twelve numbered data sets, court records, Freedom of Information Act records, and earlier DOJ disclosures. Previously released materials have also been consolidated into these sections.

A separate congressional release on September 2, 2025 made 33,295 pages of DOJ-provided records available through the House Oversight Committee. This is a distinct release; its page count should not simply be added to DOJ's total because collections can overlap. [House Oversight release and access links](https://oversight.house.gov/release/oversight-committee-releases-epstein-records-provided-by-the-department-of-justice/).

## Reading the records carefully

- A person's appearance in a record, contact book, or flight log does not by itself establish criminal conduct. Distinguish allegations, witness statements, investigative leads, and court findings.
- DOJ cautions that its production includes public submissions that may contain fabricated or false material. Publication is not independent verification of every claim. [DOJ announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-publishes-35-million-responsive-pages-compliance-epstein-files).
- Search results may be incomplete or unreliable, especially for handwritten documents. Check the underlying record and its context. [Library search limitations](https://www.justice.gov/epstein).
- The records concern sexual abuse and can contain sensitive information. Protect survivors' identities and avoid reproducing exposed personal information. DOJ acknowledges possible inadvertent disclosures and provides a reporting contact on its library page. [DOJ privacy notice](https://www.justice.gov/epstein).
